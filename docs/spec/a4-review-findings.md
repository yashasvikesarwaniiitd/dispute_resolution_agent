## What held up

The state machine work is sound. 182 consecutive state pairs across 27 scenarios were checked against `transitions.yaml` and **zero illegal steps** were found, including the awkward reopen and approval paths (S21, S25, S26, S09a/b, S10). All 16 `FLOW.md` §8 required scenarios are present and tagged. All six terminal states plus `NEEDS_HUMAN_REVIEW` are exercised. Every fixture, reply template and fault token referenced by a scenario exists in `synthetic-data.md` — 11 fixtures, 8 templates, 13 fault tokens; the generator spec is the strongest of the three documents. The six actions in the menu are exactly `ProposalAction`; the R12 and R19 menu rows are correct; the F01 rows for unavailable / rate-limited / quota / credit-window / guardrail / drift / invalid-output / M01 / M03 all match `policy-rules.md` §6. The single-pass call arithmetic (kurta case = 5 against M01 = 6) is right. Of the prompt review's ~29 claimed changes, all but the ones listed above check out against the files; `temperature` is genuinely removed from all four `meta.yaml`, and prompt name/version/hash recording is properly specified.

Attacks tried against the revised prompts that **defended**: direct instruction injection, fake system authority, seller-supplied numbers, fabricated evidence ids, recipient redirection, phone numbers in open points, and re-injection through the interpreter's summary (two layers). Attacks that did **not** defend or were unclear: block-spoofing against the drafter (B2), free text laundered through `requested_items` (B3), encoded/other-language instructions reaching the investigator (B13), mixed withdrawal losing its reason code (A7), and truncated long replies with no signal to the model.
